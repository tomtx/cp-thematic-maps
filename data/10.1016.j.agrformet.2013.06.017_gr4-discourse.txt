The spatial patterns of the component elasticities ?(E Trans, P) and ?(E Soil, P) are shown in Fig. 4 , in geographic coordinates (upper panels) and also in (T, P) coordinates defined by mean annual temperature and mean annual precipitation (lower panels). Fig. 4 shows that the highest elasticities of transpiration to precipitation occur mainly in semi-arid (P <600mmy-1) environments, most of which also have mid-range temperatures (T between 15 and 25°C). The spatial patterns of the component elasticities ?(E Trans, T) and ?(E Soil, T) in geographic and mean (T, P) coordinates (Fig. 5 ) are quite different to the corresponding patterns for elasticities to precipitation (Fig. 4), with the largest elasticities for temperature occurring in wet conditions where ET is primarily energy-limited, and the smallest in warm, dry conditions where ET is strongly water-limited. As in Fig. 4, there is substantial small-scale variability in ?(E Trans, T) and ?(E Soil, T), some of which is in opposing directions.
